AI strategy & workflow architecture

Practical AI adoption for real businesses — mapped to the work your team actually does, built with guardrails, and measured in hours saved rather than demos given.

Start a conversation

If you're new to this

You've probably heard that AI should be saving your business time, and you may have tried a chatbot and found it underwhelming. That's normal. The useful version is quieter: it handles the typing, summarizing, and re-formatting your people do every day, inside the tools you already have, with rules about what it may and may not do. We find those tasks, set it up, and teach your team, and we'll tell you plainly where AI won't help.

If you do this for a living

Workflow mapping to the task level with time and failure points; routing design across models by job (long-context reasoning, fast drafting, retrieval) with explicit handoffs and human checkpoints; reusable prompts, skills, and templates versioned and documented; operating rules covering data handling, review-before-send, and audit trail; built inside the subscriptions the company already holds wherever practical to avoid per-token billing surprises. Experience base: AI-assisted quoting, pipeline intelligence, and technical design in a live commercial sales role, with the guardrails written before policy required them.

Who it's for
Owners and operators of small and mid-size companies, sales and customer-service teams, operations leads, and professionals who have been told to “use AI” without being told how.

What "practical" means here

Practical AI adoption starts with a boring question: where does the time go? Not where could AI be impressive — where are people retyping, re-summarizing, re-formatting, and re-checking. Those are the hours a model can give back reliably. Everything else is a pilot that will quietly die.

The second question is what can go wrong. A tool that drafts customer quotes needs a check before anything ships. A tool that reads your inbox needs rules about what it may act on. We write those rules first, because a workflow without them either gets banned by leadership or burns someone. The tools draft, sort, and summarize; the judgment stays with your people, and the design makes sure of it.

Multi-model systems, in plain terms

No single model is best at everything. One is better at long technical reasoning, another at fast drafting, another at search. A multi-model workflow routes each step to the right tool and passes the work between them with clear handoffs. It sounds complicated; built properly it looks to the user like one assistant that is simply good at its job. We design the routing, the handoffs, and the checks — and we keep it inside tools your company already pays for wherever possible.

How an engagement runs

  1. Observe. We sit (virtually) with the people doing the work and map the real process, not the org-chart version.
  2. Prioritize. You get a ranked list of candidate workflows with honest estimates of hours saved and effort to build.
  3. Build. We build the first one or two with your team, in your tools, and write the operating rules alongside.
  4. Hand off. Training, documentation, and a check-in a month later to see what drifted.

Tell us where the time goes. We'll tell you where AI can give it back.
