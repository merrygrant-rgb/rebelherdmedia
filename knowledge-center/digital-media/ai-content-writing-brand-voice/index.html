AI Can Write Your Content — But Can It Sound Like Your Business?

AI content writing for small business: what it's good at, where it falls flat, and the division of labor that keeps your voice in it.

By Merry Grant, Founder & Principal

The short version

  • AI is a genuinely useful writing tool. I use it every day, and I’m not about to pretend otherwise.
  • What it can’t do is know your customers, your stories, or the way you talk. That part is still yours.
  • The businesses getting real value from AI content treat it like a fast first draft — then a human who knows the business makes it true.

Four minutes, grammatically perfect, sounded like nobody

AI wrote a blog post for me last week in four minutes. It was grammatically perfect and sounded like absolutely nobody.

Every sentence was correct. Every paragraph was organized. And there wasn’t a single story, opinion, or scrap of warmth in the whole thing. It could have belonged to any business, in any town, selling anything.

Let me put it in potluck terms. Bringing AI-written content to your customers without your voice in it is like bringing a store-bought pie to a church potluck and passing it off as homemade. Nothing wrong with the pie. But folks can tell — and nobody asks for the recipe.

What AI is genuinely good at

I’m not here to scare you off AI. That would be strange, considering I teach businesses how to use it responsibly — and I run my own company with it.

Here’s how I currently divide the work:

  • ChatGPT helps with quick drafts, brainstorming, and rewording sentences that are fighting me.
  • Kimi helps me audit and fine-tune writing, check style, and pressure-test content before publication.
  • Claude, whom I call Fable, helps me develop and organize client packages.
  • Gemini helps with images.

Right tool, right job — same as any toolbox in the truck.

AI content writing, used well, gives a small business three things it never had before: a first draft in minutes instead of days, a cure for the blank page, and the ability to turn one idea into an article, an email, and a week of social posts.

That’s real. I’d be cheating my clients if I said otherwise.

Where it falls flat

Now the straight talk.

AI has never met your customers. It doesn’t know that Mrs. Henderson calls every March about the same thing, or that your regulars would riot if you changed the Tuesday special.

It has no scar tissue from the job that went sideways, no opinion about the thing your industry keeps getting wrong, and no grandmother whose skillet taught it anything.

So it averages.

It writes the middle of the internet — polite, correct, beige.

And if your competitor down the road opens the same tool and types the same prompt, they’ll get roughly the same article you did.

For a big corporation, that’s a branding problem. For a small business, it’s an existential one.

You can’t out-spend the big folks. Your whole advantage is that customers know you, trust you, and hear you when they read your words.

Beige gives that away.

One more thing, because I teach responsible AI use and the rule is simple:

AI can help you say it, but you’re responsible for what it says.

It will occasionally state something wrong with complete confidence. Somebody who knows the business has to check.

The division of labor that actually works

Let AI do the typing.

Outlines, first drafts, research summaries, turning one article into five social posts.

You keep the talking.

The stories, the opinions, the promises, the local knowledge — anything you’d actually say to a customer.

Then apply the only editing test that matters: read it out loud.

If you wouldn’t say it to a customer sitting across the table, it doesn’t ship.

Fix the sentence until it sounds like you — that usually means making it shorter, plainer, and a little braver.

And here’s my favorite math: if AI saves you three hours of drafting, spend twenty of those minutes making the piece yours.

That’s still two hours and forty minutes back in your week.

A before-and-after you can hear

AI version:

“In today’s competitive digital landscape, leveraging artificial intelligence for content creation can optimize marketing outcomes.”

Human version:

“AI can write your first draft while you finish your coffee. Whether it should talk to your customers unsupervised is another question.”

Same idea. One of them sounds like a person you’d do business with.

The bottom line

You don’t have to choose between fast and genuine.

Let AI handle the typing; keep yourself in the talking.

Efficiency might get you noticed. Being someone customers trust is what gives them a reason to come back.

And if you’re staring at a pile of AI drafts that all sound like nobody — that’s fixable.

Sometimes it takes one read-aloud pass. Sometimes it helps to have a second set of eyes that does this for a living.

Small business supporting small business is my favorite kind of work.
