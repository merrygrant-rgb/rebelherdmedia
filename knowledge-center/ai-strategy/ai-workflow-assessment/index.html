Your Business Doesn't Need More AI Tools — It Needs a Better AI Workflow

Why the cure for AI overwhelm usually isn't another subscription — it's an AI workflow assessment: an honest look at how your work actually moves.

By Merry Grant, Founder & Principal

The short version

  • If AI hasn’t lightened your workload, the problem probably isn’t the tool. It’s the workflow underneath it.
  • A monthly subscription to AI you barely use isn’t an investment. It’s a donation.
  • Map how work actually moves through your business first. After that, the right tools get obvious — and sometimes the right answer is fewer of them.

Let me save you some money right up front

Another AI tool is not what your business needs.

I’ve noticed two very different approaches to AI among the business owners and professionals I talk with.

The first group has a whole stack of AI subscriptions — one for writing, one for images, one for notes, one somebody recommended on a podcast — and opens maybe two of them.

The second won’t touch AI at all, because the whole thing feels like drinking from a firehose.

Different problems, same root: nobody looked at the workflow first.

Here’s a truth from my grandma’s kitchen.

Her cast-iron skillet turns out perfect cornbread every single time — not because the pan is magic, but because she’s worked the same recipe, the same careful way, for decades.

The tool serves the process. Never the other way around.

AI works exactly the same.

Which brings me to what I actually do for a living.

What an AI workflow assessment actually is

Plain-English version: we look at how work moves through your business before we talk about any tools at all.

We map the workflow.

Where does work enter your business? Who touches it? In what order, and where does it stall?

Most owners have never seen their own process on one page. It explains a lot.

We audit the tools.

Everything you’re paying for, what overlaps, and — here’s the uncomfortable part — what nobody’s opened in a month.

Paying recurring fees for AI you don’t use continuously just doesn’t make sense.

If you only need it twice a month, we find a smarter way to get the same result.

You get a fix list.

Prioritized and in plain words: what to do first, what to ignore, and where AI genuinely helps versus where it’s just glitter.

Then we talk.

You get a written summary and a scheduled call with a real human.

That’s the whole point of how I work — real people solving real problems.

Sometimes the answer is a better tool. Sometimes it’s fewer tools. Sometimes it’s using what you already pay for — on purpose.

My job as an AI architect is to show you how to get what you want and need from AI, not to sell you more of it.

For the folks who’d rather not deal with AI at all

If that’s you, I understand.

The headlines are a lot — datacenters, politics, debates about where all of this is going.

I’m not here to argue any of that.

But here’s the gentle truth: AI is already in your day.

It’s in your Google searches and your Amazon cart. It filters your spam and routes your maps.

The question was never really whether AI touches your business.

It’s whether you get any benefit from it — or just everybody else does.

You don’t have to love it.

You just shouldn’t be paying for it — in subscription fees or in wasted hours — without getting something back.

Two things to do this week (before you hire anyone)

One: list every tool and what it costs.

Every subscription on one page, monthly total at the bottom.

Circle anything you haven’t opened in thirty days.

Most owners have never seen that number, and it tends to focus the mind.

Two: map one broken process on paper.

Pick the task that eats the most time.

Write out each step, who does it, and where it stalls.

Pen and paper. Fifteen minutes.

Those two pages are worth more than any software demo ever given.

And if you bring them to a conversation with someone like me, we skip the small talk and get straight to useful.

The bottom line

Your business doesn’t need more AI tools.

It needs a clear picture of how the work flows — and then tools chosen on purpose, used on purpose.

Get the workflow right, and the cornbread takes care of itself.

Describe what’s eating your time in plain words — no tech-speak required — and start a conversation.
