How sound masking actually works

Sound masking is not noise cancellation. It is a carefully shaped, barely noticeable background sound — think a quiet HVAC hush — tuned to the frequency range of human speech. Raising that background a few decibels in the right bands shrinks the distance over which a conversation is intelligible, often from forty feet to about fifteen. People stop overhearing, and they stop being overheard.

Done well, nobody notices the system. Done badly — wrong spectrum, emitters too sparse, one zone bleeding into a quiet room — people complain about a "hiss" and the system gets turned off. The difference is almost entirely in the design and the commissioning, which is why those are the two things we insist on controlling.

Direct-field or in-plenum, and why it matters

There are two broad approaches: emitters that face down into the room through the ceiling tile (direct-field), and loudspeakers that fire upward into the plenum so the sound filters down through the ceiling (indirect). Each has a right answer depending on ceiling type, plenum depth, HVAC layout, and budget. We've designed both, we're certified on the leading direct-field platform, and we'll tell you which one your building wants rather than which one we prefer.

How an engagement runs

  1. Scoping. You send floor plans (PDF or CAD) and a few photos. We talk for twenty minutes about what the space is for and what's going wrong.
  2. Design. You receive the layout, bill of materials, and notes. One revision round is included.
  3. Bid support. If you're sending it out to installers, we answer their technical questions so the bids come back comparable.
  4. Commissioning. After install, we provide the target levels and walk your installer through verification, remotely.
